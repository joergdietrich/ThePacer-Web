## Language

English or German, on screen and spoken. When you change it, the app checks that your phone has an offline voice for the new language. If it has none, an amber line says so; tap it to download one.

## Units

Metric or imperial. This changes how every distance and pace is **shown**: the training screens, the summary, history and the comparison. It does not change your sessions. A session keeps the units you defined it in, in the session form, in its name and in the spoken countdown.

## Height

Your height gives a first-ever session a better starting guess at your stride. Once you have run, the app goes by your own stride, and changing your height has no further effect unless you also reset your stride.

## Reset my stride

The app learns how your stride changes with pace and keeps what it learned for the next session. If the beat seems consistently wrong for you — after an injury, new shoes, or someone else using your phone — **Reset my stride** clears it. The app asks first, and the reset takes effect at your next session.

## Heart rate strap

The Pacer records heart rate from a Bluetooth chest strap. Any strap that sends standard Bluetooth heart rate works, which is nearly every strap sold in the last ten years. ANT+ alone is not supported.

1. In Settings, tap the strap line to open the strap screen.
2. Put the strap on and wet its contacts, so it wakes up.
3. Choose your strap from the list. Android may ask for permission to find nearby devices.

From then on the app connects to that strap for every session. The strap line in Settings shows **Connected**, **Connecting** or **Not in reach**.

Heart rate is **recorded, not shown, during a run**. Your eyes belong on the pace. You see it afterwards in the summary, per rep and for the whole run, and it goes into the .fit file.
