The Pacer runs on Android phones. During each rep it plays a beat to step to, at the cadence that puts you on your target pace. To set that beat, it learns your stride from two things every phone has: GPS for your speed, and a motion sensor for your steps.

## First-run setup

The first time you open the app, it asks three questions, one per page. You can change every answer later in [Settings]({{ '/manual/settings/' | relative_url }}).

1. **Language.** English or German. The setup switches language as soon as you choose. If your phone has no offline voice for that language, an amber line says so. Tap it to open your phone's speech settings and download the voice; without it, the spoken cues stay silent. The beat plays either way.
2. **Units.** Metric or imperial. This sets how distances and paces are shown everywhere in the app.
3. **Height.** Optional. Your height gives the beat in your very first session a better starting guess at your stride. After that the app learns your stride from your own running, so you can skip this page.

## Permissions

The app asks for permissions when you open a session's **Start training** screen, not before:

- **Location** gives distance and pace. You can refuse it and still run, for example on a treadmill; distance and pace then stay blank.
- **Notifications**, on Android 13 and later, let the app show its notification while a session runs. Android uses that notification to keep a session alive with the screen off.
- **Nearby devices** (Bluetooth) is asked for only when you pick a heart rate strap.

The app does not ask for activity recognition. It counts your steps with the motion sensor, which needs no permission.

## On a run

Carry the phone on your body: in a pocket, on an armband or in a belt, where it can feel your steps. Use earphones or a loud enough speaker to hear the beat. Once you tap Start you can lock the screen. The session keeps running, the beat keeps sounding and the cues keep coming, and Android shows a notification for as long as the session lasts.

Some phones close apps in the background to save battery. If a session stops when the screen is off, look in your phone's battery settings for an option to leave The Pacer unrestricted.
