## The beat

During every rep, The Pacer plays a steady beat. **Put one foot down on each beat, and you will run at your target pace.** That is the whole idea of the app: you listen to your pace instead of looking at it.

The beat starts with the rep, so you are on pace from the first stride rather than finding it halfway through. It mixes with your music without stopping it, and it keeps going with the phone locked in your pocket. A recovery gets a beat too if you gave it a target pace.

The tempo comes from your own stride. Runners take longer steps as they run faster, so the cadence for a given pace differs from runner to runner. The app measures your steps and your speed while you run, learns your stride, and keeps what it learned for the next session. Your very first session starts from a typical runner, so give the beat a few minutes to settle. If it ever seems wrong for you, you can reset what the app has learned in [Settings]({{ '/manual/settings/' | relative_url }}).

## Start training

Before you start, the Start training screen shows the session phase by phase, with a big **Start** button and status lines at the bottom.

<figure>
  <img src="{{ '/assets/img/screens/06-start-training.png' | relative_url }}" width="390" height="844" alt="Start training with GPS ready.">
  <img src="{{ '/assets/img/screens/15-start-training-no-gps.png' | relative_url }}" width="390" height="844" alt="Start training while waiting for GPS.">
</figure>

- **GPS signal ready** means you can go.
- While the phone has no GPS signal yet, the GPS line flashes. Wait for it, or **double-tap Start** to run anyway, for example on a treadmill. Distance and pace stay blank until a signal arrives, so a distance rep shows a **Next** button for you to tap at its end.
- If your phone has no offline voice for the app's language, a voice line appears. Tap it to download the voice.

The session starts the moment you tap. Lock the phone, put it away, and step with the beat.

## The training screens

You do not need to look at the screen while you run; the beat and the voice tell you what you need. When you do look, each phase has its own screen, titled with its name. During reps and recoveries a counter such as **3 / 6** tells you which rep you are on.

<figure>
  <img src="{{ '/assets/img/screens/07-training-warmup.png' | relative_url }}" width="390" height="844" alt="An open-ended warm-up with the Next button.">
  <img src="{{ '/assets/img/screens/08-training-rep.png' | relative_url }}" width="390" height="844" alt="A rep, with the remaining distance and the pace gauge.">
</figure>

- **Remaining**, in the large card, is what is left of the phase, with a progress bar.
- **Time elapsed** and **Distance elapsed** count the current phase.
- **The pace card** on a rep shows the [pace gauge]({{ '/manual/pace-gauge/' | relative_url }}), your current pace, the target and your average for this rep.
- **An open warm-up** shows a big **Next** button. Tap it when you are ready for the first rep, and the beat begins.
- **The cool-down** shows a big **Finish** button, which ends the session.

Phases with a length move on by themselves.

## The spoken cues

Near the end of every rep and recovery, the large card turns green and a voice counts you down.

<figure>
  <img src="{{ '/assets/img/screens/18-training-countdown.png' | relative_url }}" width="390" height="844" alt="The countdown card at the end of a rep.">
</figure>

- **The countdown** marks the last part of a phase in seconds or metres, and ends with every second from 5.
- **The next phase** is announced as it begins, with its target pace: "Repetition 4 of 6, four thirty per kilometre". The beat for the new pace follows.

Music is lowered while the voice speaks, and a podcast or audiobook pauses and then carries on. A phone call silences both the voice and the beat.
