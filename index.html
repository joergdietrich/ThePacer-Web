Step to the beat, run on pace

The Pacer is a running metronome. For every rep it plays a beat at the cadence that puts you on your target pace. Put a foot down on each beat, and the pace follows from the first stride, without a glance at your watch.

Why run intervals

An interval session alternates hard reps with easy recoveries: six times 400 metres with a jog between, say, or five times three minutes. The recoveries let you spend far more time at a hard pace than you could hold in one continuous run, and that time at pace is what drives the training effect.

A bigger engine

Reps near your top aerobic speed are one of the most effective ways to raise your VO2max, the ceiling on how much oxygen your body can use.

Holding a harder pace

Longer reps at threshold pace teach your body to sustain speed before fatigue sets in, which is what races from 5 km to the marathon are decided on.

Speed and economy

Running fast with good form, rep after rep, makes every pace cheaper, and an hour of intervals buys more fitness than an hour of easy running alone.

The hard part is the pace

An interval session is a prescription: this distance, at this pace, this many times. Its benefit depends on running it as written, and that is harder than it sounds.

Right from the first stride

Rested legs and adrenaline make the first rep feel easy, so most runners start too fast and pay for it in the last reps. On a short rep there is no time to settle in: by the time you notice you are off, it is half over.

Every rep the same

A session run too fast becomes a different, harder workout, and one run too slow misses its point. Holding the same pace on the sixth rep as on the first takes constant small corrections.

Eyes on the wrist

With a watch, the only way to know your pace is to look, again and again. Every glance breaks your form and your focus, and the number on the screen trails behind what your legs are doing.

Not everyone has a track

Marked laps make pacing easier, but most runs happen on roads and paths, where your only reference is what a device tells you.

The beat does the pacing

Your pace is your stride length times your cadence, the number of steps you take a minute. The Pacer knows your stride, so it can turn any target pace into a cadence and play it as a beat. You stop watching your pace and listen to it instead.

1. Set a target pace

Define your session once: the reps, their pace, the recoveries. 4:30 per kilometre, say.

2. Hear the beat

As each rep starts, The Pacer plays the beat for that pace. It mixes with your music and keeps going with the phone in your pocket.

3. Step with it

One foot down on each beat. You are on pace from the first stride, and you stay there to the last rep.

A beat that fits your stride

Runners lengthen their stride as they speed up, and no two runners do it the same way, so a fixed tempo cannot fit everyone. The Pacer measures your steps and your speed while you run, learns your stride, and sets the beat from it. After a few minutes the beat is yours, and the app keeps what it learned for the next session.

Built around the beat

The beat paces you. Everything else in The Pacer keeps you on the session you planned, with your eyes up.

Spoken cues

A voice counts down the end of every rep and names the next one and its pace. Music dips under it, and the beat carries on.

Works in your pocket

Lock the phone and put it away. The session keeps time, the beat keeps sounding and the cues keep coming.

A gauge when you want one

If you do look, a needle shows at a glance whether you are fast, slow or on pace, with the same in words.

Compare week by week

Every run is kept. Run the same session again and compare it rep for rep with the last five times you ran it.

Heart rate

Pair a Bluetooth chest strap and the summary shows your heart rate for every rep.

Your runs stay on your phone

No account and no cloud. A run leaves the phone only when you share its .fit file with Strava, Garmin Connect or anything else.

Every rep, on the record

When you finish, the summary rates every rep against its target and counts how many were on pace. Run the session again next week, keep to the beat, and watch the same pace come at a lower heart rate.
